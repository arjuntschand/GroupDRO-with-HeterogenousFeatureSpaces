What this says:

- **The current λ learning rate was too small to do anything.** λ does not leave its initialisation, so by Appendix E those runs are not valid DRO runs.
- **Larger helps, up to a point, and the refresh frequency matters more than the step size.** Per-step refresh engages λ at every γ; per-epoch needs γ ≥ 0.5 before it moves.
- **On NHANES the middle of the range buys about two points of worst-group accuracy and nothing else.** The 3-seed table above suggested every measure improved at once; at 10 seeds (table below) the accuracy gain holds and is significant for three of four arms, while worst-group loss, AUROC and class-balanced accuracy are unchanged. It does not hurt them either, which is what separates γ 0.5 from γ 2.0.
- **γ = 2.0 overshoots.** On NHANES it has the highest accuracy and the lowest AUROC, worst loss and lowest class-balanced accuracy: the model has drifted toward predicting the majority class, and λ is one-hot within five epochs. An earlier version of this section called it validation-selected on NHANES; that selection was made on the same drift-prone accuracy and does not stand.
- **On Fed-Heart the effect is small.** The classes are balanced there, so accuracy is a fair measure, and the only significant change in the table is Regret-DRO at γ 2.0 (+0.97, p = 0.010; +1.10, p = 0.028 on seeds not used to choose the cell). For the full method nothing is significant, and its best worst-group loss is at γ 0.1 per-step.

**Recommendation.** Use **γ = 0.1 with per-step refresh** as the paper's default: λ engages on both datasets, the full method has its best worst-group loss on both (0.570, 0.484), and it costs no accuracy anywhere. (γ 0.1 at 10 seeds on NHANES is still running; the recommendation will be re-checked against it.) γ = 0.5 per-step is equally defensible and is the better cell for Regret-DRO without anchors. Do not use γ = 2.0 on NHANES. The draft's refresh period N = 50 steps sits between our two cadences (about once per epoch on NHANES, once per six epochs on Fed-Heart), so N should be stated per dataset or set relative to steps per epoch.

`fig10_recommended_dynamics_ours_regret.png` shows held-out loss and λ against epoch for the current setting beside γ 0.5 per-step: λ has moved 0.02 and 0.00 at the reported epoch in the first, 0.47 and 1.70 in the second, after which it runs to one-hot and the up-weighted group's held-out loss climbs. Validation-based epoch selection stops before that.

Not covered: EMBED. Its sweep used the p_g initialisation, under which λ cannot move at 1:1000 imbalance (Appendix E), so it needs the same 1/G + per-step treatment before its DRO rows mean anything.

Why the collapse goes to Switzerland on Fed-Heart: the eq. 13 margin is largest for the smallest group, which pushes Switzerland's floor to 0.105 while its held-out loss sits near 0.28, so it shows the largest excess by construction. That is the conservative reference "erring on the safe side", and it is safe for exclusion, but with an engaged max player it also decides where the weight goes.

Three things follow.

- At the paper's setting (γ = 0.02, per-epoch) λ moves 0.004–0.010 by the reported epoch. By Appendix E those runs are invalid; the reported gain over per-group ERM there is the 1/G (or p_g) weighting itself, not the max player.

- Worst-group accuracy stays within 73.3–75.0 across the whole grid. The reason is structural rather than a tuning failure: on Fed-Heart the worst-accuracy group is VA, which also has the highest floor (0.511). Regret is designed to de-prioritise a group near its floor, so worst-group accuracy cannot improve under regret here. Worst-group loss is the quantity the objective moves (best 0.563 at γ = 0.5 per-step, regret).

Read this on loss, not accuracy. Starving G2 makes G2's *accuracy* go up for every arm (our arms 72.9 → 82.5, REMIND 75.4 → 84.4) while its *loss* goes up too (REMIND 0.499 → 1.278): with 10.5% positives, a data-starved group drifts toward majority prediction, which raises accuracy and ruins calibration, and the worst group flips from G2 to G0. So the +5 on "worst-group accuracy" for our arms is the metric changing which group it measures, not a gain. AUROC on the starved group falls for every arm, and falls *most* for the anchored arms (G2 AUROC full → A: PerGroupOnly 0.809 → 0.739, GroupDRO 0.808 → 0.743, Ours_GDRO 0.803 → 0.670, Ours_Regret 0.805 → 0.669). So there is no evidence here that the anchors let a starved rich group borrow strength; if anything the anchored arms give up more discrimination on it. What is robust is the loss column: the anchored arms' worst-group loss rises modestly (0.536 → 0.578) while the three modality-sharing baselines' roughly doubles (1.17–1.29). Lemma 1's borrowing mechanism is not visible in this experiment.

Regime B at 10 seeds (our arms; baselines still 3 seeds until their run finishes) shows the same artefact in the other direction. Starving G0 raises G0's accuracy for every arm (PerGroupOnly 68.1 → 74.6, Ours_Regret 76.2 → 80.2) while G0's AUROC falls (0.77 → 0.74 and 0.76 → 0.72) and the worst group moves to G2, so "worst-group accuracy" rises across the board (PerGroupOnly 66.76 → 74.24). On loss the arms separate: the non-anchored per-group arms' worst-group loss jumps (PerGroupOnly 0.553 → 0.701, GroupDRO 0.531 → 0.611, RegretDRO 0.513 → 0.612) while the anchored arms hold (Ours_GDRO 0.536 → 0.540, Ours_Regret 0.524 → 0.541). On AUROC of the starved group the anchored arms again give up slightly more than the plain per-group arms. So across both regimes: accuracy is unusable for this experiment, loss favours the anchored arms, AUROC on the starved group does not.

## 7. Latent geometry: 2-Wasserstein distances

eq. 16 W₂ between diagonal Gaussians fitted to the test latents, divided by the mean squared latent norm (the anchors shrink the space ~40×, so unnormalised distances read as alignment when they are only shrinkage). NHANES nested, GroupDRO on, 10 seeds:

| arm | latent scale | W₂ between groups, same class | W₂ between classes, same group | class / group ratio | worst-group acc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no anchors | 14.03 | 2.568 | 0.208 | 0.37 | 70.16 |
| real anchors | 0.357 | 0.152 | 0.510 | 3.53 | 72.54 |
| random anchors | 0.259 | 0.234 | 0.496 | 2.42 | 72.46 |

Without anchors the latent space is organised by group, not by class. Real anchors pull groups 17× closer and classes 2.5× further apart. Random anchors do nearly as well and score the same accuracy, so the class-conditional structure is real in the geometry but is not what carries the benefit. That sharpens Appendix B's wording: the structure exists; the benefit does not depend on it. Figure: `fig7_latent_w2.png`.

The picture version is `fig11_latent_scatter.png`: every NHANES test patient as a point in each model's latent space (first two principal components), coloured by group on top and by outcome underneath. Without anchors the three groups occupy three separate regions and the first component, 98% of the variance, is group identity, so the shared head is looking at three different populations. With class anchors the groups overlap and the first component becomes the outcome axis, with the two anchors at its ends. Randomly assigned anchors overlap the groups almost as well, which is the control result in one glance.

The same picture for Fed-Heart is `fig11_latent_scatter_fedheart.png` (the 150 test patients of one split), and it explains why the anchors help on NHANES and not there. Without anchors the four hospitals already overlap (scale-normalised W₂ between groups 0.65, against 2.57 on NHANES) and the outcome already runs along the main axis, because the hospitals record largely the same 10–13 variables and their encoders land in similar regions unprompted. The anchors tighten the groups only modestly (0.65 → 0.42; randomly assigned anchors do not, 0.65) and otherwise add a constraint, which is consistent with their costing about two points of worst-group accuracy on Fed-Heart. Alignment is what the anchors buy, so they pay off where the groups are misaligned to begin with.

**Is the class separation in the picture all there is?** Yes. 99–100% of the gap between the class means lies in the plotted plane, so the projection hides nothing. Separating NHANES test patients along the anchor-to-anchor axis gives AUROC 0.77, which is the model's own test AUROC, and every arm including shared-encoder ERM sits at 0.78–0.80: the classes overlap because CVD is only that predictable from these variables. Raising the anchor weight does not change it (NHANES, seed 42):

| anchor weight | class gap / cloud spread | AUROC along anchor axis | W₂ between groups (norm.) | worst-group acc | per-class acc [no CVD, CVD] | worst-group class-balanced acc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 (paper) | 0.86 | 0.772 | 0.198 | 70.8 | 72.5 / 72.6 | 71.3 |
| 1.0 | 0.86 | 0.781 | 0.102 | 75.2 | 77.7 / 65.6 | 68.3 |
| 1.0, per-(group, class) | 0.85 | 0.772 | 0.070 | 76.2 | 79.9 / 62.3 | 64.9 |
| 10 | 0.99 | 0.781 | 0.065 | 78.4 | 83.2 / 51.7 | 61.8 |

A 100× stronger pull tightens the groups further and leaves class separation and AUROC where they were. Worst-group accuracy does rise with the weight, but that is the operating point sliding toward the majority class: recall on CVD falls from 72.6 to 51.7 and class-balanced worst-group accuracy falls from 71.3 to 61.8. It is the same artefact as in item 5, and it applies to the anchors' NHANES accuracy gain in general: in the 10-seed table the anchored arms have the higher worst-group accuracy (72.3–72.6 vs 70.2) but the lower class-balanced worst-group accuracy (61.1–63.8 vs 65.4) and no higher AUROC (0.78–0.79 for all). NHANES claims about the anchors should be made on AUROC, loss and the alignment distances, not on accuracy. The EMBED version is `fig11_latent_scatter_embed_main.png` (25,632 test exams, 500 plotted per group), and it shows a different failure. The anchors pull the six group centroids onto one point (W₂ between groups 1.03 → 0.19), but the four class anchors end up only 2.45 apart, 31% of the latent scale and closer together than the class means they are meant to organise (5.84 apart). On NHANES and Fed-Heart the anchors sit 125–181% of the latent scale apart, further than the class means, so the fit term pulls the classes apart; on EMBED it squeezes them together, which fits the anchors costing 4.5 points of worst-group accuracy there. The EMBED latent space also barely shrinks (scale 13.9 → 8.0, against about 40× on the tabular datasets), and the EMBED runs train 20 epochs at learning rate 5e-5, so the likeliest reading is that its anchors are under-trained rather than that anchors cannot work on images. A separate, larger learning rate for the anchor parameters is the obvious thing to try. A two-column main-text version (no anchors, class anchors) is `fig11_latent_scatter_main.png`.

## Alignment moments per (group, class), as eq. 6 specifies

The paper runs pooled the class moments over groups. Re-running Fed-Heart with per-(group, class) moments (1/G init, eq. 13 floors, γ = 0.02, **10 seeds × 5 folds**) changes nothing: every arm moves by 0.3 points or less with p > 0.4 (AnchorsOnly 71.20 → 71.45, Ours 72.15 → 71.90, Ours_Regret 72.10 → 72.40; Shared_Anchors 68.99 → 67.98, p = 0.10). The eq. 6 form is neutral on Fed-Heart.
